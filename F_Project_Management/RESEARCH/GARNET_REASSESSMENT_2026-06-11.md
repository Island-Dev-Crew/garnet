**Inputs read in full or extracted in depth:** nine language-creator transcripts (Hejlsberg, Gjengset, Peyton Jones, Ryhl, Stroustrup, DHH, Kelley, Ronacher, Lattner), the Trajectory Research report, the Dual-Mode TL;DR analysis, W_REBUILD_SPEC, the S129–S200 Command Center, plus fresh same-day web verification of the 2026 regulatory, insurance, and agent-economy landscape.

## 0. Verdict

The thesis holds, and it holds harder than it did a week ago. Three of the nine builders — without knowing Garnet exists — independently described Garnet's exact problem as the unsolved problem of their field:

- **Stroustrup** described the regulated-software AI crisis verbatim: *"There's regulatory bodies, there's validation. You have to be able to validate what you changed when you make a change. And the AI tools... even if you make a slightly different prompt, a lot of the code will change and you have to now check it again — all of the code that was generated... When a human makes a change, it's localized and you can look for the effects. If an AI writes it, you don't actually know where it's changed."* He added that senior developers in his field are **retiring rather than validate AI churn**. That is the diff-caps problem stated by the creator of C++. Garnet's answer: the textual diff may be enormous, but the *authority and bounds diff* is machine-computable and small — that is the localization instrument for AI-generated change.
- **DHH** reported that Amazon's internal analysis of its major outage concluded they *"can no longer let junior programmers ship agent-generated code to production without review,"* and that all agent acceleration is accruing to seniors *because only seniors can validate*. The senior-validation bottleneck is the review bottleneck wearing a different shirt. Garnet's pitch sharpens: **package a slice of the senior engineer's validation judgment into a deterministic gate**, so juniors and agents can ship inside a proven envelope.
- **Peyton Jones** (the genuinely new voice in this corpus) independently validated the static-evidence-for-agents thesis: static types are *"a huge boon for LLMs"* because they *"cut down the space of programs the LLM can generate"* and let *"a zillion iterations get done behind the scenes."* And he supplied the deployment framing Garnet should adopt: Stroustrup chose static typing because *"a telephone switch can't drop into a debugger."* **An autonomous agent is the telephone switch** — software operated with no human at the screen is exactly the software that needs compile-time evidence, not runtime archaeology.

Nothing found in this pass invalidates the current plan. The consolidation PR, FLEET-TOOLING PR, and W-REBUILD order stand as written. What this pass adds is (a) five architecture-grade lessons from the builders that should be folded into the RB slices and the spec, (b) seven verified gaps in the prior research — the largest being a complete absence of the regulatory layer that goes live in **three months**, (c) three unclaimed use cases that nobody owns as of today, and (d) a concrete answer to the library question. Under-claim, over-deliver, as always.

## 1. What the Nine Builders Teach

Each subsection closes with the directive(s) Garnet should absorb. The Kelley, Ronacher, and Lattner extractions were already deep in the Dual-Mode TL;DR; they are refreshed here only where this pass surfaced something new.

### 1.1 Anders Hejlsberg (Turbo Pascal, Delphi, C#, TypeScript)

C# was born as deliberately *both*: "the power and expressiveness of C++ with the ease of use of Visual Basic." Twenty-five years of survival is existence proof that the dual-promise pattern — Garnet's "Rust rigor, Ruby velocity" — works **when it ships as one coherent surface**, which is exactly the graduated-syntax collapse already decided. TypeScript is the deeper lesson: Hejlsberg didn't replace JavaScript, he *fixed* it, and the static type system existed explicitly **to power tooling** — "that's ultimately where the productivity comes from... once you give it to them, you can never take it away again." And C#'s first hit features (properties, events) were *first-class treatment of what everyone was already doing ad hoc*.

→ **Directive 1:** Garnet's caps are types-for-tooling, one level up: TypeScript's types exist to power the IDE; Garnet's caps exist to power the *acceptance gate*. Market it that way — "TypeScript made JavaScript tractable for large teams; Garnet makes code tractable for agent teams."
→ **Directive 2:** @caps is first-class treatment of what the whole industry already does ad hoc (IAM policies, permission manifests, allow-lists scattered across YAML). That ad-hoc-to-first-class move is the historically proven adoption pattern.
→ **Directive 3:** Once a team has lived with the diff-caps gate, they can never go back to reviewing raw diffs of agent code. Design for that irreversibility — the gate must be pleasant enough to become an entitlement.

### 1.2 Jon Gjengset (Rust educator; Helsing; ex-AWS Rust build system)

Four load-bearing findings. First, **adoption barrier ≠ learning curve**: "I actually think adoption is not that related to the learning curve... the biggest barrier is momentum in existing companies" — switching cost, retraining, codebase inertia. Garnet's zero-switching-cost zone is greenfield agent-authored code, where none of those forces exist; the four-language converter attacks the remainder. Second, **the clap lesson**: an entire adoption category (Rust CLIs) was created substantially by one excellent library. Third, the **typestate coordinate-frame library**: a library with "almost no implementation code" whose entire value is what the type system makes inexpressible-to-get-wrong — and which "couldn't exist in Python." This is the trust-artifact delta test in its original form. Fourth, the **senior/junior bootstrap problem**: "you need the one or two senior people first that you can then grow a team around" — companies are capped on juniors by seniors. His AI skepticism ("I don't think it can understand") is already answered in the TL;DR: Garnet gates on evidence, not on model understanding.

→ **Directive 4:** Pick Garnet's clap (see §6, Libraries): one category-defining library, and the recommendation is the MCP/agent-tool server library — building an attested tool server in Garnet should be the easiest way to build one in any language.
→ **Directive 5:** Adopt the senior-multiplier framing everywhere: Garnet doesn't replace senior judgment; it *amortizes* it. One senior defines the envelope once; the gate enforces it on every junior and every agent thereafter.

### 1.3 Simon Peyton Jones (Haskell; GHC; Verse at Epic) — new to the corpus

The richest single source in this pass. Five lessons:

1. **One inviolable principle.** Haskell survived 35 years by never trading its single core principle (purity/immutability — "we're just not going to give you unrestricted side effects, sorry") while improving everything around it. "Avoid success at all costs" — don't compromise the principle to chase users, and don't let early success freeze the design. Garnet has the discipline (calibrated honesty) but has never stated its principle in one sentence. Proposed, for the top of the README and the spec: **"No authority without evidence. Acceptance is a decision made on evidence the author cannot fake."** Everything else — syntax, modes, stdlib — is negotiable; that is not.
2. **The typed Core IR.** GHC's Core is a tiny (~3 datatypes, 8 constructors), 35-year-stable, *statically typed* intermediate language — and GHC re-typechecks Core **after every optimizer pass** to catch compiler bugs at the pass that introduced them instead of as a segfault three stages later. SPJ: "I know of no other production compiler that has this property." This is a direct architectural gift to Garnet: **make capabilities part of the core IR's type system and re-check the caps invariant after every lowering pass.** Then "the seal attests what the core proves" is mechanically true through the entire pipeline, and a future backend (R5) cannot silently launder authority during codegen. This should be written into the R2/R4 rowan-unification acceptance criteria and into the R5 backend memo as a hard constraint.
3. **The agent is the telephone switch.** Static typing was chosen for software with no human at the screen. Agent-operated software is permanently unattended. Use the line.
4. **Maintainability is the killer benefit of static evidence**: SPJ refactors a 35-year-old compiler "fearlessly because the type system keeps me safe." In the agent era, *the agent* performs the refactor and *the human* needs the fearlessness — caps/bounds invariance across a refactor is what makes an agent refactor of legacy code acceptable.
5. **The principled escape hatch.** Haskell's `unsafePerformIO` is long, ugly, named-unsafe, and shifts the proof obligation to the human; its `Dynamic` type is the disciplined fallback where static typing can't reach. Garnet's managed↔@safe boundary and its FFI story should copy the shape exactly: the escape hatch exists, is loud, is greppable, and transfers obligation explicitly.

→ **Directive 6:** Adopt the one-sentence principle. → **Directive 7:** Typed core IR with caps, re-checked per pass — fold into RB-4 criteria and the R5 memo. → **Directive 8:** Steal "copilots need pilots" + "the agent is the telephone switch" for the site's threat-wall narrative.

### 1.4 Alice Ryhl (Tokio maintainer; Rust-for-Linux; Google Android)

Four directly portable mechanisms. **Editions** — "the way Rust makes breaking changes without breaking people," with per-crate opt-in and full mix-and-match interop — are precisely the migration vehicle for Garnet's dual-surface collapse: ship the graduated syntax as a new edition, keep old-surface modules compiling forever, never re-run Python 3 (Ronacher's ten-year wound, §1.7). **Doc-examples-as-tests** ("if you change the underlying code, your test fails... you can't forget to update your examples") generalizes beautifully: every @caps and @bounded claim in documentation should be a compiled, trap-tested example — drift between docs and enforcement becomes a build failure, which is the xtask-truth idea extended from numbers to semantics. **Unsafe-as-encapsulated-extension** ("as long as you design your API right, you can add new language features by using unsafe") is the formal pattern for the binding ring in §6: FFI is Garnet's unsafe; a binding is safe iff the unsafe is encapsulated behind a caps-audited API. **The RFC template** (motivation → guide-level explanation → reference-level explanation → rationale/alternatives/prior-art) plus final-comment-period governance is the lightest-weight credible process to adopt the day Garnet has its second contributor. Two bonus signals: Torvalds' distrust of cargo ("downloads code from the internet and runs it") is the supply-chain anxiety Garnet's sealed registry answers at the highest level of the stack; and Ryhl independently lands on the thesis — "agents can talk to the compiler... Rust could be a pretty promising candidate for agents because they can get more feedback."

→ **Directive 9:** Editions for the surface collapse — write it into the RB-4 plan now so the collapse never requires a flag-day. → **Directive 10:** Caps-claims-as-doctests in the truth-guard family. → **Directive 11:** The pre-registered governance pattern (RFC template + FCP) goes in a GOVERNANCE.md stub before the first external contributor arrives, not after.

### 1.5 Bjarne Stroustrup (C++)

Beyond the §0 quote, four lessons. **"Don't mess with the linker"**: 25 linkers existed at Bell Labs; he refused to touch any of them and used C as the portable assembler — "we can have Dennis's mistakes, which we know, or my mistakes, which we don't know yet." That is the integrate-don't-rebuild doctrine stated in 1983, and it is the strongest possible endorsement of the R5 lean (Cranelift/Wasm as Garnet's "C"). **The problem test**: "What you need is a problem that needs a solution... most of the time that can be done reasonably well with existing languages" — the same bar Lattner sets; Garnet's answer must stay crisp (the acceptance gate requires semantics a linter can't retrofit: capability propagation through the type system, bounds as language contracts, a core IR that preserves both). **Profiles** — his current work, "enforced guidelines" per domain — are the closest C++ ever gets to @caps/@stability, and worth crediting as prior art the way Austral is credited. **Negative-overhead abstraction**: more compile-time information lets the optimizer beat hand-written low-level code; Garnet's caps and bounds annotations are *optimizer-legible facts* (a @bounded loop with a declared ceiling is a vectorization gift) — a performance story for later, but worth one line in the spec so it isn't designed out. And the shipping discipline: "languages designed to be perfect for future computers mostly die — by the time the world gets the language, the world has changed." Plus the regret that he shipped before templates and "got saved by luck" with the STL — don't launch with the library story half-baked (§6).

→ **Directive 12:** Quote "Dennis's mistakes which we know" in the R5 memo. → **Directive 13:** Credit C++ Profiles in the prior-art ledger. → **Directive 14:** The library ring must be real before the public launch wave (W-LAUNCH gate), not after.

### 1.6 DHH (Ruby on Rails; Omarchy; 37signals)

The conversion story matters as market evidence: a prominent skeptic flipped to agent-first in weeks once the capability threshold was crossed, and his framing of the result — "stepping into a mech suit... I have been hyper-accelerated as a programmer" — is the demand curve Garnet rides. Three specifics: the **Amazon finding** (§0) is the acceptance crisis at hyperscaler scale, citable. The **100-PRs-in-90-minutes** review session (agent-assisted triage of Omarchy's backlog) shows review itself going agentic — which means the *gate* must emit evidence an agent reviewer can consume, not just a human; diff-caps output should be designed as both a human one-glance artifact and a structured machine verdict. The **crowded-market encouragement**: "there's about 7,000 different Linux distributions... all the ideas in the world may be taken and it doesn't matter, because your spin on it isn't" — the correct emotional answer to the "every pillar has prior art" concession. And one honest tension to log: DHH claims Rails is now "one of the most token-efficient ways of building web apps," while the Alderson benchmark in the TL;DR found heavyweight-convention frameworks cost agents the most. Both can be true — Rails is concise to *write* and expensive to *iterate blind* — and the synthesis is exactly Garnet's lane: expressive surface (Ruby's gift) **plus** static feedback (what Rails lacks). Joy and evidence, not joy versus evidence.

→ **Directive 15:** Diff-caps output gets a `--machine` mode (structured verdict JSON) from day one — the reviewer on the other side of the gate is increasingly an agent. → **Directive 16:** Keep "joyful always": DHH is proof that the people who feel the acceleration most are the people who care most about aesthetics, and they choose tools they love.

### 1.7 Refresh: Kelley, Ronacher, Lattner

**Kelley (Zig)** adds one sharpening this pass: "you want to avoid a dependency for your *core product*" (Zig's LLVM divestment) — which looks like a contradiction of integrate-don't-rebuild and isn't. ## 2. Gaps and Holes in the Prior Research (verified against today, 2026-06-11)

The Trajectory Research report remains excellent on its chosen ground — capability systems, bounded execution, provenance, diffing. The holes are in what it never looked at:

**Gap 1 — The regulatory layer is entirely absent, and it goes live in three months.** The report contains no mention of the EU Cyber Resilience Act, whose Article 14 reporting obligations apply from **11 September 2026** — 24-hour early warning and 72-hour notification of actively exploited vulnerabilities through ENISA's Single Reporting Platform, covering software products already on the EU market, with fines up to €15M or 2.5% of global revenue — and whose full obligations (SBOM, secure-by-design conformity assessment, CE marking, technical documentation) apply 11 December 2027. The CRA also pulls legacy products into scope upon "substantial modification" — meaning *proving a change did not substantially modify the security posture* now has direct legal value. A language whose compiler emits the capability manifest, SBOM, bounds profile, and signed provenance as ordinary build outputs turns a consultant-driven cost center into a `garnet build --evidence` flag. The US side (CISA Secure-by-Design, the memory-safety directives Ryhl cited) is the same wind. **This is the single largest positioning omission in the prior research**, and the timing is a gift: Garnet's evidence pipeline ships into a market that becomes legally obligated to want it the same quarter.

**Gap 2 — The agent-governance standards wave of early 2026 postdates the report.** Since the research was compiled: NIST launched an AI Agent Standards Initiative (February 2026) spanning interoperability, security, and testing, with an RFI on AI Agent Security and an **AI Agent Identity and Authorization concept paper**; Singapore shipped the first national governance framework for agentic AI (January 2026) with **"risk bounding"** as a named pillar; OWASP published a Top 10 for Agentic Applications. The standards bodies are converging on exactly Garnet's vocabulary — bounding, authorization, attestation — with no reference implementation. The "seed a standard" recommendation in the prior research now has named venues and a clock.

**Gap 3 — The consumption side of evidence is unexamined.** The research covers how to *emit* trustworthy artifacts (in-toto predicates, SBOM compatibility) but never asks who *reads* them and in what workflow: policy engines (Kyverno/OPA gates), CI checks, insurers, auditors, regulators, and — per DHH — agent reviewers. Each consumer needs a different rendering of the same seal. Treat the evidence bundle as a product with multiple front-ends (one-glance human view, structured machine verdict, regulator-format annex).

**Gap 4 — Memory primitives and typed actors were never assessed.** The research audits the five trust pillars exhaustively and says nothing about Garnet's other two marquee features. Honest preliminary read: memory primitives compete with a fast-moving framework layer (the Letta/MemGPT/LangGraph lineage) where language-level fixation is risky, and typed actors have deep prior art (session types, Pony, typed Akka, BEAM). Neither weakens the trust thesis; both need their own prior-art-and-novelty pass before they appear in launch messaging. Until then, the trust kernel leads and these ride as "runtime contract" features, not headlines.

**Gap 5 — Runtime identity for agents.** The research covers attestation of *artifacts*; it never touches identity of the *operator* — which workload identity (SPIFFE/SPIRE-class) and now NIST's agent-identity work address. The seal should be designed to bind to an agent identity claim when one exists (who ran, under whose delegation), without inventing the identity layer itself. Integrate, don't rebuild — again.

**Gap 6 — The report's "Garnet not independently verifiable" caveat is now resolvable and unreconciled.** It was written before the full-source audit. Several of its recommendations have since been *independently converged on* by the engineering work (compile-bounded-to-fuel ≈ the R5 Wasmtime lean; wrap-Sigstore ≈ the seal already being CycloneDX+GPG-shaped), and that convergence — research and implementation arriving at the same answers separately — is itself evidence worth one paragraph in Paper-series material. A short reconciliation pass (research recommendation → repo reality → delta) belongs in the consolidation PR's documentation set.

**Gap 7 — Competitive watch has no refresh cadence.** Nothing in the corpus tracks new entrants in the agent-native-language space, agent-sandbox runtimes, or attestation startups on a schedule. One recurring slice (quarterly is enough) keeps the white-space claim honest: the moat is integration + timing, and timing requires a watch.

## 3. Three Unrealized Use Cases — Problems With No Owner as of Today

Constraint honored: these exclude everything already on the books (the three flagship demos, the capability transparency log, caps→sandbox compilation, agent provenance in the seal, the cross-language manifest standard). Each was checked against today's landscape; the honest claim is "no prior owner found," with the nearest neighbors named. Each is also deliberately **a future skin over the existing kernel** — none changes the W-REBUILD plan; all three fall out of diff-caps + bounds + seals once those are real, which is itself confirmation the kernel is pointed at the right star.

### 3.1 Delta-Certification: Pre-Authorized Change Envelopes for Regulated Software Maintained by Agents

**The problem nobody has solved:** Regulators have already invented the *demand*. The FDA's Predetermined Change Control Plan (final guidance Dec 2024, extended Aug 2025) lets a medical-device maker pre-authorize a *class* of future modifications — and the compliance condition is exactly an envelope test: the manufacturer "stays within the bounds of an authorized PCCP when it only implements modifications contemplated by the plan" and any change outside the envelope triggers a full new submission. Automotive OTA (UNECE R156), avionics (DO-178C change impact analysis), and the CRA's "substantial modification" trigger all encode the same shape: *change is permitted if and only if it stays inside a pre-approved envelope.* But the envelope test today is performed by **humans reading documents** — and Stroustrup told us what happens when AI-velocity change meets human-document validation: the seniors retire, or the changes stop. There is no mechanism, anywhere, that *machine-verifies a code change's membership in a certified envelope.*

**Why Garnet uniquely:** an envelope is precisely what Garnet's kernel emits — a capability surface + a bounds profile + a sealed baseline. Delta-certification is then: `garnet diff-caps --envelope cert/PCCP-2026-001.envelope` returns INSIDE (authority surface ⊆ certified surface, bounds ⊆ certified ceilings, intended-use caps unchanged, trap tests green) or OUTSIDE (here is the widening, escalate to full review). The textual diff can be ten thousand agent-written lines; the *certification-relevant* diff is one screen. No linter on Rust or Python can do this, because the envelope must be a compiler-enforced semantic object, not a convention. The deliverable becomes: certify the envelope once with humans; verify every subsequent change mechanically, forever.

**Industries:** medical devices, automotive, aerospace, rail, energy/grid control, industrial automation, and — via the CRA — eventually all software sold into the EU. **What it needs from the roadmap:** nothing new before v0.8.2. It is flagship demo #3 (safety-critical trust artifact) grown into a market: the demo's artifact *is* an envelope; the only addition is an `--envelope` comparison mode on diff-caps, a natural post-v0.8.2 slice. **Nearest neighbors found:** PCCP tooling consultancies (documents, not mechanisms); SBOM-diff tools (inventory, not authority).

### 3.2 Underwritable Software: Capability Envelopes as the Actuarial Instrument for Agent Liability

**The problem nobody has solved:** the AI-agent liability insurance market is forming *right now* — Mount (YC, 2026) sells liability policies for companies deploying autonomous agents, and its underwriting method is telling: they "scan and red-team AI deployments, quantify operational risk... and insure the residual." Red-teaming is point-in-time, behavior-sampled, and expensive — because **there is nothing attestable to underwrite against.** Legacy cyber insurance prices questionnaires; agent insurance prices red-team snapshots; neither has an instrument that says, verifiably and continuously, *what the insured system is capable of doing.* When a loss occurs, liability allocation between model vendor, agent operator, and the human who accepted the code is adjudicated from logs and depositions.

**Why Garnet uniquely:** a sealed capability + bounds envelope is an actuarial instrument. The insurer underwrites the envelope, not the codebase: premium priced on declared authority surface (does it touch payments? PII? prod credentials?) and ceilings; policy conditioned on the diff-caps gate staying green (any accepted widening is a *notifiable change in insured risk*, automatically); claims adjudicated by a mechanical question — was the loss-causing behavior inside or outside the attested envelope? — with the seal chain assigning *who accepted which authority when*. That last property is the liability firewall the whole agent economy is missing, and the same artifact serves Singapore's "risk bounding" pillar and the EU AI Act's traceability requirements for free. Insurance is also the quiet adoption flywheel: nobody mandates a language, but insurers discount for evidence, and a 15% premium delta has moved more engineering practice than any manifesto.

**Industries:** insurance/reinsurance, fintech risk, enterprise procurement (vendor security review collapses to envelope inspection), legal/e-discovery. **What it needs:** nothing before v0.8.2; afterwards, a two-page "envelope as insurable instrument" brief and one conversation with an agent-insurance carrier. The trust artifact already exists — this names its buyer. **Nearest neighbors found:** Mount and peers (market, no instrument); model-performance insurance à la Armilla (insures the model, not the code's authority).

### 3.3 Metered Delegation: Compositional, Attenuating Compute-and-Spend Budgets Across Agent Swarms

**The problem nobody has solved — calibrated carefully, because the money half is being addressed:** Google's AP2 (Sept 2025, 60+ partners, v0.2 April 2026), Stripe/Tempo's MPP, and Coinbase's x402 give agents cryptographically signed *payment mandates* — spending limits enforced at the **wallet/rail layer**. Three things remain unowned. First, **non-monetary resources**: an agent swarm's token spend through the org's own API keys, CPU-seconds, fan-out amplification, and recursion depth have no mandate layer at all — and runaway-swarm spend is the FinOps incident class of 2026. Second, **attenuation across delegation in code**: when agent A delegates to B and C, AP2's mandate does not subdivide; the protocol explicitly punts agent identity and delegation to other layers. Macaroons solved attenuation for bearer credentials a decade ago; no language has it as call-graph semantics. Third, **first-line enforcement**: the wallet bounces a violating transaction *after the program tried it*; nothing today makes the program *provably incapable of attempting* it.

**Why Garnet uniquely:** Garnet already has the primitives nobody else has as language syntax — @rlm_budget, @fan_out, @max_depth, @bounded — and the caps lattice already propagates through the call graph. Metered delegation is the unification: budgets become a resource lattice that **attenuates monotonically through delegation** (a child's budget is carved from, and can never exceed, its parent's — XOR-diffable exactly like diff-caps), checked at compile time where decidable, fuel-metered at runtime where not, and *sealed* so the swarm's total resource authority is an auditable artifact. An AP2 mandate then becomes the boundary value of the lattice: the wallet enforces the perimeter; Garnet proves the interior. Budget-widening becomes an acceptance event in the same gate as capability-widening — one gate, two lattices.

**Industries:** cloud FinOps, AI platform teams, grid/data-center capacity planning (attested worst-case draw per workload), sustainability reporting, and agentic commerce itself (interoperate with AP2, own everything above the rail). **What it needs:** the RLM guardrails already in the spec, generalized post-v0.8.2 into the budget lattice; the AP2-interop note is one paragraph in the spec's future-work section now, so the design doesn't foreclose it. **Nearest neighbors found:** AP2/MPP/x402 (money only, rail-layer only); cloud quotas (infra-level, non-compositional, don't travel with the artifact); Wasmtime fuel (mechanism without mandate semantics).

**Near-miss worth logging (not counted in the three):** sealed computational science — a Garnet artifact as the executable, attested methods section (data caps showing exactly which datasets were touched, deterministic bounds, full provenance) for the reproducibility crisis in computational research. Real, unowned, but smaller and slower than the three above; park it in the opportunity ledger.

## 4. Top 10: Problems Other Languages Own That Garnet Can Solve Better

"Better" is claimed honestly throughout: better *for the agent era* — same job plus evidence — never "better codegen than Rust" or "bigger ecosystem than Python." Ranked by (market size × evidence-advantage × near-term feasibility). Each entry: incumbent → what they solve → Garnet's edge → what it requires.

**1. CI/CD pipeline steps and build-system plugins** (incumbents: Bash/YAML+JS GitHub Actions, Groovy/Gradle, Jenkins). CI code executes with god-credentials and near-zero review — the exact substrate Shai-Hulud weaponized, and where 796 packages got backdoored with *valid* signatures. A CI step written in Garnet carries a compiler-derived caps manifest: this step provably cannot read the signing key, cannot reach the network, cannot touch paths outside `dist/`. The action marketplace becomes inspectable at the authority level. *Requires:* the diff-caps gate (flagship #1) plus a thin Actions adapter — among the cheapest, highest-leverage skins on the kernel.

**2. Data pipelines and ETL glue** (incumbent: Python, totally). Pipelines are the #1 category of code agents now write, and they touch production data by definition. Python cannot express "this transform reads `orders` and cannot reach the network"; Garnet makes data authority (`@caps(data.read: pii.customers)`) a typed, diffable, sealed property — exfiltration becomes a compile error and a refused diff rather than an incident. *Requires:* the binding ring's data tier (§6).

**3. Infrastructure-as-code and policy** (incumbents: HCL/Terraform, Pulumi, CloudFormation). IaC is *pure authority manipulation* written in languages with no authority types; `terraform plan` diffs resources, never capabilities. Garnet-expressed infrastructure diffs as an authority delta — "this change grants the billing service write access to the user store" as the plan's headline — which is what every cloud security review is trying to reconstruct by hand. *Requires:* cloud-provider bindings; medium lift, enormous prize.

**4. Serverless and edge functions** (incumbents: JS/TS and Python on Lambda/Workers). Multi-tenant platforms already meter and sandbox at the infra layer; Garnet moves the contract into the artifact — per-invocation @bounded ceilings and caps that *are* the deployment policy, with the R5 Wasm lean making the compile target and the enforcement mechanism the same thing (Wasmtime fuel). The platform pitch writes itself: upload evidence, not promises. *Requires:* R5 decision in the Wasm direction.

**5. Plugin and extension ecosystems** (incumbents: JS for browser/editor extensions, Lua for game/app embedding). Every extension store reviews hand-written permission manifests an attacker can lie in; Garnet manifests are compiler-derived and sealed — the store verifies, not trusts. This generalizes flagship #2 (MCP attestation) to every host-and-guest ecosystem. *Requires:* flagship #2 shipped; the generalization is documentation plus one host SDK.

**6. Long-running services and agent orchestration** (incumbents: Erlang/Elixir/BEAM, Go, typed Akka). BEAM owns fault tolerance for processes; nothing owns fault tolerance for *agents*. Garnet's typed actors + compiler-checked protocols + mailbox/recursion bounds + budget lattice (§3.3) is OTP for the agent era: supervised agent trees where a child cannot exceed delegated authority or budget by construction. *Requires:* the actor layer hardened post-v0.8.2; honest sequencing — after the trust kernel, not beside it.

**7. CLI tools** (incumbents: Rust, Go — the clap effect). Agents both write and *run* CLIs; an installed tool is ambient authority on the operator's machine. Garnet CLIs install with their manifest (`brew info` shows the authority surface) and run with enforced bounds — plus the REPL/joy work (R7) makes Garnet genuinely pleasant here. *Requires:* R7 + one excellent args-and-caps library (Garnet's clap, possibly the same library as Directive 4's tool-server play).

**8. Embedded and OTA-updated device software** (incumbents: C, Rust). Concede codegen and ecosystem maturity to Rust outright. Garnet's wedge is the *update acceptance* path: the device (or its fleet manager) verifies that incoming firmware's sealed authority surface didn't grow — peripherals, buses, network — before flashing. This is delta-certification (§3.1) on metal, and it composes with Rust rather than fighting it (Garnet gate, Rust firmware, attested boundary). *Requires:* envelope mode on diff-caps; a partner-shaped play, not a solo one.

**9. Financial and transactional agents, on-chain-adjacent** (incumbents: Solidity, Move; Rust for infra). Move's linear resources protect assets *on* chain; the off-chain agent layer that decides to move money has nothing. Garnet agents carrying AP2 mandates as typed caps (§3.3) make the deciding code as auditable as the settling contract. *Requires:* budget lattice + AP2 interop; post-v0.8.2.

**10. Scientific and analytical computing reproducibility** (incumbents: Python/R/Julia). The languages own the math; none owns the *trust* in the result. Sealed runs — data caps, deterministic bounds, full provenance — make "rerun my exact analysis" a verification, not an archaeology. Smaller market, long fuse, natural academic allies; ranked last on feasibility-now, not on worth.

The pattern across all ten, stated once for the website and the papers: **other languages solve the execution problem in each domain; none solves the acceptance problem; Garnet solves acceptance everywhere code arrives faster than trust.** And per DHH's 7,000-distros encouragement — every one of these categories is crowded, and none of them has Garnet's spin.

## 6. The Library Question, Answered: The Core Ring

**Is it still the case?** Yes. Ecosystem remains the weakest score on the board (distribution 2/10, community 0.5/10), and Ronacher's testimony is the mechanism: language choice *is* ecosystem choice — "unless I would have built everything from zero, it wouldn't have worked." Stroustrup's biggest stated regret is shipping before the library story was ready and being "saved by luck." Garnet does not get to rely on luck.

**The strategy — turn the constraint into the differentiator.** Garnet cannot and should not chase npm-scale. The right target is Deno-std-scale: one curated, in-repo, sealed set — call it the **Core Ring** — of roughly 20–30 bindings that cover ~80% of agent-era tasks. The doctrine assembles from three builder lessons already extracted: Alice Ryhl's unsafe-encapsulation pattern (FFI is Garnet's `unsafe`; a binding is admitted when the unsafe surface is encapsulated behind a caps-audited safe API), Kelley's core-product rule (the ring is *not* the core product, so it integrates mature Rust crates — ring/rustls, serde, sqlite — rather than rebuilding them; the *audit and the manifest* are the Garnet-original part), and the research's own FFI finding (every capability system in history breaks at the FFI, so the library strategy and the caps-soundness strategy are the same strategy: the Ring **is** the audited FFI boundary, and nothing crosses it unmanifested).

**What makes it a moat rather than a chore:** every Ring binding ships with a compiler-derived caps manifest, an FFI-audit note, trap-tested doc examples (Directive 10), and a seal. The tagline writes itself: **the only standard library where every function's authority is declared and verified.** Gjengset's stale-library complaint becomes Garnet's contrast story — small, maintained, attested versus vast, abandoned, ambient.

**Contents, by tier (agent-task-profile driven, per Ronacher's "ecosystems are good at specific things"):** Tier 1 — serialization (JSON/YAML/TOML), HTTP client+server, regex, time, fs, proc, crypto/hashing. Tier 2 — sqlite + one network DB (postgres), templating, markdown, logging/trace. Tier 3 (the differentiating tier) — model-API clients (Anthropic/OpenAI), **the MCP/tool-server library** (Directive 4 — Garnet's clap, the category-defining one), and the AP2-mandate type stub (§3.3). Tier 3 is where the Ring stops being parity and starts being the reason to come.

**Sequencing — this is the part that costs nothing to decide now:** the Ring lands **after RB-3**, because RB-3's `#[garnet_primitive]` macro infrastructure is exactly the binding factory — registry-derived dispatch makes adding an audited binding a declarative act instead of a 2,500-line bridge-file edit. So: RB-3 → Ring Tier 1 as a named post-v0.8.2 workstream (it slots naturally into the W-SHIP synthesis) → Tier 3's MCP library co-developed with flagship demo #2, which needs it anyway. Per Directive 14, Tier 1 + the MCP library are a **W-LAUNCH gate condition**: no public launch wave on an empty shelf.
