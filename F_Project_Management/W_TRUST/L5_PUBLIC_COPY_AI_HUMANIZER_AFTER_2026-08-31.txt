## Origin
Garnet began with a question: if a machine writes the code, what must the human accepting it be able to verify? DHH supplied the spark in [DHH’s new way of writing code](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/dhhs-new-way-of-writing-code) on The Pragmatic Engineer, published April 8, 2026. The idea moved from thought to machine around March-April 2026.
The loop closed publicly on August 26, 2026, with Lex Fridman Podcast [#501 – DHH: Future of Programming, AI, Agentic Engineering, Vibe Coding & Linux](https://lexfridman.com/dhh-2/). Those two conversations bracket Garnet's origin: make authority visible to the person accepting agent-written code, then make the traps match the claim ([U-72](F_Project_Management/W_TRUST/AUGUST_2026_ARC_REGISTER_SWEEP_2026-08-27.md#u-72--origin-record-correction-the-spark-was-dhh-on-the-pragmatic-engineer)).
[GitHub's language bar](https://api.github.com/repos/Island-Dev-Crew/garnet/languages) counted this repository as **38.7% Python** at the 2026-08-31 `f6d3285` main snapshot because the trust and verification harness is Python; the product compiler and runtime are Rust.

  <h1>Garnet v0.4.2, and the case for evidence accounting</h1>
  <p><strong>v0.4.2 is live.</strong> The release — checksum-verified macOS Apple-Silicon tarball, Linux <code>.deb</code>/<code>.rpm</code>, and <code>SHA256SUMS</code> — was published on 2026-05-15 and the universal installer pulls and verifies it end to end. You can install it in about a minute: see <a href="/getting-started.html">Getting Started</a>, or the <a href="https://github.com/Island-Dev-Crew/garnet/releases/tag/v0.4.2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">v0.4.2 release</a>.</p>
  <p>That is the easy half of this post. Here is the more interesting half.</p>
  <h2>Two numbers that point in opposite directions</h2>
  <p>Garnet now tracks two different progress signals, and in this cycle they moved in opposite directions:</p>
  <p>The <strong>tracked implementation ledger is 87/87 (100%)</strong>. Every slice in the v0.5 readiness plan — parser parity, managed runtime, the actor/Sendable bridge, safe-mode ownership hardening, trait/generic coherence, Memory Core, and the release/installer gates — has executable evidence and is checked off. That is real, and it is verifiable: <code>python3 scripts/garnet_readiness_status.py</code>.</p>
  <p>The <strong>MIT readiness pulse went down — from 58.6% to 55.8%</strong>.</p>
  <p>A headline number went down in a release announcement. That deserves an explanation because it is the central fact on this page.</p>
  <h2>Why the number dropped</h2>
  <p>The MIT pulse is meant to estimate distance to a genuinely production-grade, MIT-quality language and toolchain — not distance to "the current sprint is done." The Windows and Linux distribution lane (signed installers, native packaging beyond the current assets, platform smoke beyond the tarball path) was real, mostly open work that the aggregate simply <em>was not counting</em>. Adding it to the denominator lowered the percentage.</p>
  <p>We could have left it uncounted and watched the number drift up. We didn't, because a metric you curate upward is not a metric — it is marketing with a progress bar. The evidence-matched move was to make the lane visible and let the aggregate fall to where the truth actually is.</p>
  <blockquote>The implementation ledger answers "did we finish what we scoped?" The MIT pulse answers "how far is this from something you'd bet a company on?" Both are true at once. Conflating them is how research prototypes get oversold.</blockquote>
  <h2>What "research-grade prototype" keeps meaning</h2>
  <p>Garnet v0.4.2 is a <strong>research-grade prototype, not a production-complete language</strong>. Signed macOS <code>.pkg</code> and Windows MSI artifacts are not built yet. The compiler-as-agent memory work, native backend, mechanized proofs, and empirical validation are open research lanes, not shipped guarantees. The site says so in the comparison table's last row, on the install page, and now here. That framing will not soften as the numbers climb — it is part of the contract with anyone evaluating this.</p>
  <h2>What actually shipped</h2>
  <p>Beyond the release itself: the public site was rebuilt for current truth and first-run success — a prerequisite/checksum callout, a Rust-vs-Ruby-vs-Garnet positioning table, a real first-project terminal (the output is captured, not faked), a <a href="/getting-started.html">Getting Started</a> walkthrough, an explicit Community section (no Discord theater), and accessibility/SEO basics. Every change went through the same dogfood-readiness gate the language uses on itself.</p>
  <p>If you want the pulse of the project between posts, the <a href="https://github.com/Island-Dev-Crew/garnet/commits/main" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">commit history</a> and <a href="https://github.com/Island-Dev-Crew/garnet/releases" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">releases</a> are the ground truth. This blog will carry the narrative ones — and it will keep reporting numbers that occasionally go down.</p>

    <div class="eyebrow">Positioning note · August 2026</div>
    <h1>Article 14 starts a clock. Evidence has to keep up.</h1>
    <p class="dek">The EU Cyber Resilience Act's Article 14 reporting obligations begin to apply on September 11, 2026. The deadline is concrete. So is the question behind it: what changed, what authority moved, and what can the producer prove?</p>
  </header>
  <div class="notice"><strong>Positioning, not launch.</strong> Garnet is a research-grade v0.x project. It is not a CRA compliance product, legal advice, a conformity assessment, or a promise tied to September 11. No delivery date is implied here.</div>
  <section>
    <h2>What Article 14 puts on the clock</h2>
    <p>Article 14 covers actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe security incidents for products in scope. Its early-warning deadline is no later than 24 hours after awareness; the next notification is due no later than 72 hours after awareness. The final-report timetable differs between vulnerabilities and incidents. Full application of the Regulation follows on December 11, 2027.</p>
    <p>That is a reporting duty, not a language feature. It does sharpen a question software teams already face: when code changes faster than a person can reread it, what can the organization prove about the authority that changed?</p>
  </section>
  <section>
    <h2>Garnet's position</h2>
    <p>Garnet treats authority as reviewable program data. <code>garnet check</code> verifies declared capability budgets across the registered surface. <code>garnet diff-caps</code> reduces a large source change to its declared-capability delta. Neither command makes a product compliant; they make the acceptance record smaller and harder for the author to fake.</p>
    <p>The proposed <code>garnet build --evidence</code> path is <strong>under construction</strong>. No such build flag exists in the shipping CLI. Today's runnable pieces are <code>garnet caps</code>, <code>garnet diff-caps</code>, and <code>garnet seal</code>; the seal records the declared surface, but it does not rerun the checker.</p>
  </section>
  <section>
    <h2>Verified today / Still open</h2>
    <div class="grid">

          <li><code>@caps</code> and <code>@max_depth</code> have deterministic traps on the interpreter and VM, with cross-OS trap parity recorded.</li>
          <li>The <code>garnet</code> CLI runtime-gates the bounded host-authority set named in the capability scope table.</li>
          <li>Linux seccomp application is proven through the external reference tests.</li>
        </ul>
      </article>

        <ul>

          <li>The named checker-only, declared-only, unbridged, and caps-invisible fences are not runtime enforcement.</li>
          <li>macOS and Windows OS-sandbox application, regulatory validation, and production readiness remain unproven.</li>
        </ul>
      </article>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section>
    <h2>Why the review ceremony matters</h2>
    <p>Evidence only matters if its author cannot grade it into acceptance. Garnet's repository keeps implementation, independent review, the structured record, carrier approval, re-evaluation, and merge as separate acts. A content change voids the earlier verdict; sanctioned record-only succession does not extend what was reviewed; a missing record stays red. Those separations matter more than the format of an evidence bundle. A generated PDF does not prove acceptance. A useful chain ties each claim to a trap and each acceptance to its decision-maker.</p>
    <ol class="sequence">
      <li><strong>Declare.</strong> Name the authority the program is meant to hold.</li>
      <li><strong>Trap.</strong> Test the bounded enforcement surface and preserve the red cases.</li>
      <li><strong>Review.</strong> Bind independent judgment to exact bytes, head, and tree.</li>
      <li><strong>Accept.</strong> Let the accountable carrier decide; do not let the producer self-promote.</li>
    </ol>
    <p>That position follows the existing register and ceremony rail ([U-32](https://github.com/Island-Dev-Crew/garnet/blob/main/ops/lane1/review/04-request.md), [U-33](https://github.com/Island-Dev-Crew/garnet/blob/main/ops/lane1/review/04-request-addendum-u33.md)). It does not upgrade either finding or claim that the open work has shipped.</p>

    <h2>Rust, Ruby, Garnet — <span class="accent">side by side, claim by claim.</span></h2>
    <p class="lead">Garnet is not claiming to replace either parent. This table is positioning, not a benchmark: where each language sits, and what Garnet's dual-mode design buys you. The last row stays tied to current evidence.</p>

### docs/why.html
          <span class="thesis-line">Regulated buyers don't want vibes. <code>garnet build --evidence</code> is <span class="ink">under construction</span>, not in the CLI today, and is designed to turn compliance into a compiler artifact.</span>
          <p><code>garnet build --evidence</code> is under construction; the shipping CLI has no such build flag. The proposed command would emit a <span class="gold">Verifiable Evidence Bundle</span> with the build: what this code is capable of, what it is incapable of, and the capability declarations those facts derive from. Producing that record can take weeks of document review today. The design is to make it a compiler command that runs on every build.</p>
          <p>That remains construction work. Today's runnable commands are <code>garnet caps</code>, <code>garnet diff-caps</code>, and <code>garnet seal</code>. No authority without evidence; no evidence without a check that proves it.</p>
          <span class="claim construction">Claim class · under construction · W&#8209;SHIP deferral; no shipping build flag</span>
          <span class="layer-a"><strong>Authority.</strong> Garnet. Capability declarations are checked by <code>garnet check</code>, with deterministic enforcement proven today on the bounded surface below. That proof is not yet wired into <code>garnet build</code>. Delegation is designed to attenuate, and the evidence flag remains under construction. Structural, not sampled.</span>
      <p class="layermap-note">Tests are empirical: they check behaviors someone thought to check. Capabilities are structural: they are built to bound behaviors nobody thought of. A swarm can pass every test in the suite while holding authority it never needed; no green checkmark speaks to that. That is why <code>garnet check</code> runs <em>inside</em> your CI, whoever's runners it uses. A faster pipeline checks the declared envelope more often. Automated evidence regeneration remains under construction.</p>
